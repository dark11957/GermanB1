## Lernziele

By the end, you can identify the dative recipient and accusative thing with a two-object verb, use the usual order for noun phrases and pronouns, and produce a clear message about giving, sending, showing or lending something.

## 1. Lesetext: Die Nachricht für Frau Demir

> **Nadia:** Kannst du mir bitte dein Ladegerät leihen? Mein Handy hat keinen Akku mehr.<br>
> **Samir:** Natürlich. Ich gebe dir das Ladegerät.<br>
> **Nadia:** Danke! Ich schicke meiner Schwester gleich eine Nachricht. Sie holt mich später ab.<br>
> **Samir:** Soll ich ihr auch eine Nachricht schreiben?<br>
> **Nadia:** Nein, ich schreibe sie selbst. Kannst du mir vielleicht ihre neue Nummer zeigen?<br>
> **Samir:** Ja, ich sende sie dir.<br>
> **Nadia:** Super. Danach bringe ich Frau Demir den Brief.<br>
> **Samir:** Gibst du ihr den Brief persönlich?<br>
> **Nadia:** Ja. Sie hat mir gestern einen Schlüssel gegeben, und ich möchte ihr heute den Brief zurückgeben.<br>
> **Samir:** Dann kannst du ihr auch die Nachricht von ihrer Nachbarin zeigen.<br>
> **Nadia:** Gute Idee. Ich lese sie ihr vor.

### Erst verstehen

1. Why does Nadia need the charger?
2. What will Nadia send to her sister?
3. What does Samir send Nadia?
4. Why does Nadia bring Frau Demir a letter?
5. In *Ich gebe dir das Ladegerät*, which phrase is the recipient, and what is given?

<details class="solution"><summary>Show reading answers</summary><ol><li>Her phone battery is empty.</li><li>A message.</li><li>Frau Demir’s new phone number.</li><li>Frau Demir gave Nadia a key the day before; Nadia is returning the letter today.</li><li>*dir* = recipient (dative); *das Ladegerät* = thing given (accusative).</li></ol></details>

## 3. Grammatik verstehen

### Two objects: the recipient and the thing

Some common verbs can take two objects: a **dative recipient/person** and an **accusative thing**. The cases are determined by the verb pattern, not by English word order. Ask *wem?* for the person and *wen oder was?* for the thing.

| Pattern | Example | Question test |
|---|---|---|
| Subject (nominative) + verb + recipient (dative) + thing (accusative) | Samir gibt **Nadia** **das Ladegerät**. | Wem gibt er es? Nadia. Was gibt er ihr? das Ladegerät. |
| Subject + verb + thing + recipient | Ich schicke **die Nachricht** **meiner Schwester**. | What is sent? the message; to whom? my sister. |

Useful verbs: *geben, schenken, schicken, senden, zeigen, erklären, erzählen, leihen, bringen, schicken, vorlesen,* and *zurückgeben*. Some verbs have additional meanings or patterns; learn each construction in context. A verb with only one object can also require dative: *Ich helfe meiner Schwester* has no accusative object.

### Word order: practical defaults

When **both objects are full noun phrases**, the dative usually comes before the accusative: *Ich gebe **dem Nachbarn** **den Schlüssel**.*

When **one object is a personal pronoun**, the pronoun normally comes before the full noun phrase: *Ich gebe **ihm** den Schlüssel.* / *Ich gebe **ihn** dem Nachbarn.*

When **both objects are personal pronouns**, the accusative pronoun normally precedes the dative pronoun: *Ich gebe **ihn ihm**.* The forms identify their roles; word order can change for emphasis or contrast, but these patterns are the neutral starting point.

| Object type | Usual order | Example |
|---|---|---|
| two nouns | dative → accusative | Sie zeigt dem Gast den Weg. |
| pronoun → noun | pronoun → noun | Sie zeigt ihm den Weg. / Sie zeigt ihn dem Gast. |
| two pronouns | accusative → dative | Sie zeigt ihn ihm. |

### How to choose the forms

Use the L04 accusative table for the thing: *der Brief → den Brief; die Nachricht → die Nachricht; das Handy → das Handy*. Use L05’s dative table for the recipient: *der Gast → dem Gast; die Schwester → der Schwester; die Kinder → den Kindern*. Pronouns also change: *ich → mir/mich; du → dir/dich; er → ihm/ihn; sie → ihr/sie; wir → uns/uns; ihr → euch/euch; sie → ihnen/sie; Sie → Ihnen/Sie*.

**Common mistakes:**

- *Ich gebe dich das Buch* → **Ich gebe dir das Buch.** The recipient is dative.
- *Ich gebe ihm es* → neutral order **Ich gebe es ihm.** Two pronouns: accusative before dative.
- Do not call every English “to” phrase dative by meaning alone. In German, check whether the verb assigns dative or uses a preposition.
- One noun phrase can be accusative without masculine article change: *Sie zeigt die Nummer.* Its unchanged article does not make it nominative.

## 4. Übungen: Controlled practice

1. Identify the cases: *Ich gebe dem Gast den Schlüssel.*
2. Complete: Samir schickt ___ Schwester ___ Nachricht. (his; a)
3. Complete: Nadia zeigt ___ die Nummer. (Samir; pronoun)
4. Replace both objects: *Ich gebe dem Kind das Buch.* → Ich gebe ___ ___.
5. Put in neutral order: **sie / den Brief / dem Nachbarn / bringt**.
6. Put in neutral order: **ich / ihm / es / leihe**.
7. Choose the recipient form: *Die Kollegin erklärt (ich / mir) die Aufgabe.*
8. Choose the thing form: *Er zeigt ihr (der Weg / den Weg).* (masculine object)
9. Replace the recipient with a pronoun: *Wir schicken sie unserem Lehrer.* (Context: *sie* refers to *die Nachricht*.)
10. Replace *meiner Freundin* with a pronoun: *Ich erzähle meiner Freundin die Geschichte.*
11. Write the two question checks for: *Mara liest den Kindern einen Text vor.*
12. Correct: *Ich leihe meiner Schwester.* (intended: I lend my sister a book.)
13. Complete the natural pattern: Ich bringe ___ (you singular) ___ (the keys).
14. Translate the roles into German: “He explains the rule to us.” (*die Regel*)

## 6. Eigene Sätze

Write four original two-object sentences with *geben, schicken, zeigen,* and *erklären*. Include one with two nouns, one with a dative pronoun and noun, and one with two pronouns. Label the recipient **D** and thing **A**.

## 7. Schreiben

Write an 80–100-word message to a friend describing how you helped someone. Include three two-object verbs and explain who received what. Use at least one sentence with a full dative noun phrase, one with a dative pronoun, and one with two pronouns.

**Assessment guide (0–2 each):** both objects are identifiable; case forms match verb patterns; pronoun/noun order is natural; message is complete and connected; register fits the friend. Accept any coherent original answer.

## 8. Sprechen und Kommunikation

**Information-gap role-play:** Person A has three items to lend and three messages to pass on. Person B asks for objects and gives instructions about recipients. Say each transaction aloud: *Kannst du mir … geben?; Ich schicke ihr …; Zeig uns …; Ich bringe ihm …* Then swap roles.

Solo alternative: choose three objects nearby, invent a recipient for each, and say one sentence with both objects. Useful repair phrase: *Wem soll ich das schicken?* / *Was soll ich ihr geben?*

## 9. Hörverstehen und Aussprache

Cover the text and have a partner read the dialogue naturally, or use German text-to-speech. Listen first for what Nadia borrows; on the second pass, list the things Nadia gives, sends or shows and their recipients. Reveal the dialogue as transcript after listening. TTS is supplementary, not authentic listening practice.

**Pronunciation:** In *gebe ich es ihm*, keep the unstressed pronouns distinct; do not swallow the final -m of *ihm*. Stress the new or contrasted information: *ICH gebe dir das Ladegerät* (I, not someone else); *Ich gebe DIR das Ladegerät* (you, not someone else). Grammar and emphasis work together.

## 10. Wiederholung und Selbstcheck

Recall the dative/accusative article forms from L04–L05. Explain why *Ich gebe ihm das Buch* and *Ich gebe es dem Mann* place the pronoun first, while *Ich gebe es ihm* places the accusative pronoun before the dative pronoun. Review tomorrow and again in a week.

- [ ] I can distinguish a recipient from the thing transferred.
- [ ] I can form both object cases.
- [ ] I can use the usual order for nouns and pronouns.

## 11. Lösungen und Lernhinweise

Closed questions have explained solutions. In translation and original production, other verbs and word orders may be correct if they express the relationship accurately and follow that verb’s case pattern. Do not exact-match open answers.
